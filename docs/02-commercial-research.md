# Jungle Tribe - Commercial diligence and venture case

Status: Preliminary research framework and modeled scenarios, NOT independently validated market demand.

## Competitive map to verify
- Direct plant-sitting marketplaces: Leaf'Em, House Plant Sitting. Validate whether operational, cities served, active listings, fees, bookings, customer reviews and differentiators.
- Adjacent care marketplaces: Rover, Wag, Taskrabbit and local plant-care specialists. Benchmark booking UX, trust, fees and cancellation handling.
- Collection/care apps: Planta and Greg. Benchmark onboarding, plant recognition, collection sharing, retention and free-tier features.

## Differentiation hypothesis
The free collection is a low-friction acquisition and trust layer; sitter collections serve as visible evidence of engagement. Specialist care, photo reporting, and targeted local liquidity may beat generic house-sitting services. This remains a hypothesis.

## Unit economics: illustrative, not forecast
Assume average booking gross value $150 and 15% commission collected from sitter: platform gross revenue $22.50 per booking, before payment fees, support, verification, insurance integration, fraud, refunds, acquisition and taxes.
- $1m annual platform gross revenue: ~44,445 bookings/year (~122/day).
- $10m annual platform gross revenue: ~444,445 bookings/year (~1,218/day).
- $50m annual platform gross revenue: ~2,222,223 bookings/year (~6,088/day).
- If a customer books twice yearly at $150, annual gross revenue per customer is $45 before costs.
- If a customer books monthly, annual gross revenue per customer is $270 before costs.
- The key venture challenge is frequency and marketplace density, not merely plant ownership prevalence.

## Model to build with validated inputs
Annual revenue = active booking customers × bookings per customer per year × average booking gross value × effective take rate.
Contribution margin = platform revenue - payment processing - refunds/chargebacks - sitter screening subsidy - customer support - trust/safety - promotions - variable infrastructure.
Calculate acquisition payback separately for sitter and customer sides, by city cohort.

## Research plan
1. Confirm direct competitors' current product, footprint, traffic proxies, pricing, and evidence of transactions. Avoid treating website existence as traction.
2. Interview 20-30 serious collectors in Nashville, plus 15-20 potential sitters. Ask about actual travel frequency, historical care arrangements, willingness to pay and access concerns, not only stated interest.
3. Launch Nashville two-sided waitlist, track qualified leads and geographic overlap; do not equate signups with demand.
4. Concierge-pilot 20-30 real paid bookings with manually matched vetted sitters before fully automating marketplace workflows; document insurance/liability terms with counsel.
5. Test pricing by duration, visit complexity, travel radius, and fees. Track repeat behavior over 90-180 days.
6. Model best/base/worst city expansion scenarios, CAC, booking frequency, and required density to reach $10m+ revenue.

## Suggested evidence gates (proposed, not industry benchmarks)
- At least 20 completed paid pilot bookings with no unresolved critical safety issues.
- Request-to-completion conversion and cancellation reasons measured, not guessed.
- At least 30% of interviewed owners demonstrate concrete buying intent (deposit or actual booking), not just waitlist interest.
- Positive variable contribution margin after actual payment and service costs.
- Repeat booking behavior and CAC payback trajectory strong enough to plausibly scale beyond seasonal vacations.

## Major risks
Low booking frequency; hyperlocal supply/demand fragmentation; expensive trust/verification; damage to high-value plants; private-home access; seasonality; insurance coverage exclusions; competitor substitutes; free collection features that attract users but fail to convert to bookings.

## Venture conclusion
Potentially attractive niche marketplace plus acquisition flywheel, but venture scale is NOT yet proven. Proceed with pilot and commercial diligence; use results to decide whether to invest in full nationwide expansion. Future plant sales/trading is strategic optionality, not included in current revenue forecasts.
